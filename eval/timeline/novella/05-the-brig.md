# Chapter 4

On the second day of the gales, an hour after she cleared Port Ellery, the Corinna began to take water forward, and by noon she was labouring so heavily that Captain Rudd ordered the pumps manned in shifts.

Tobias pumped until he could not feel his arms. The boy from Hull pumped beside him and talked about his mother's kitchen. The Welshman pumped and said nothing at all.

In the middle of the afternoon a sea came over the starboard bow green and solid, and when it had gone the Corinna had lost her mainmast. It hung over the side in a ruin of rope and canvas, beating against the hull, and every blow sounded like a door being kicked in. Rudd cut it away with his own axe.

Without the mast she would not answer. She went where the sea sent her, which was north and east, towards the Skerries.

That night, a little after the second watch was called, the Corinna struck the Skerries broadside and heeled over until her deck stood like a wall. Tobias was thrown into the scuppers and came up holding a rope he did not remember taking hold of. Somebody was shouting for the boat. Somebody else was shouting that there was no boat any more.

He climbed. It was the only thing he knew how to do. He went up the slope of the deck and over the rail and onto the rock, and he found that the Welshman was already there, holding out a hand, and that between them they could pull the boy from Hull out of the water by his collar.

There were three of them on the rock when the brig broke up. Far to the south-west, low on the horizon, Tobias could see a light going round.
