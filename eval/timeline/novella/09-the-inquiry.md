# Chapter 7

On the sixth day of the gales the wind blew itself out, and the inquiry sat in the Harbour Office at Port Ellery, in a room that smelled of ink and wet wool.

Mr Abernathy read his own letters aloud, in a voice that suggested he had not expected anyone to listen to them. The Board asked Tobias why the Corinna had sailed against advice, and Tobias said that he had been an ordinary seaman for one day and had not been consulted. Evan Price said the brig was rotten forward and everybody knew it. The boy from Hull cried and was excused.

The Board found that the Corinna had been lost by misfortune, and that Captain Rudd had acted as a seaman should, and that the keeper's daughter of Carrick Island was to receive the thanks of the Board and a sum of ten pounds.

The following morning the ferry ran again for the first time since the gales began. Tobias went aboard it at the Port Ellery steps with his father's sea-chest, which Maren had brought across for him, and he did not go home with her. He had signed on a steamer for Glasgow.

This time he stood in the stern and looked back until the quay was out of sight, and Maren, who had told him so, did not say anything at all.
