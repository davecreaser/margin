# Chapter 5

Three days after the ferry sailed, at the first grey of the morning, Maren saw smoke on the Skerries.

It was very little smoke, and it came and went with the wind, and her father said it was spray. She did not argue. She went down to the boat-house and dragged the old pilot gig down the slip by herself, which everyone on Carrick would have said could not be done, and rowed out through the broken water with the gig shipping a sea at every third stroke.

It took her two hours to cross a mile and a half. When she came under the lee of the rock she found her brother sitting on a ledge with a boy asleep against his shoulder and a dark man feeding a fire made of a ship's timbers.

Tobias looked at her for a long moment.

"You'll say you told me so," he said.

"I'll say it when we're home," said Maren. "Get in the boat."

She took all three of them off, the boy first, and rowed back with the wind behind her, and it was easier, though she did not say so. Their father was standing on the slip when they grounded, with a blanket over each arm and a third round his own shoulders, and for the first time since her mother died Maren saw him weep.
