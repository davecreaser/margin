# Chapter 5

Two days after the bridge fell, the inquest sat in the long room of the Fleece, because there was nowhere else in Wendle with enough chairs.

Constable Pike gave his evidence standing, as he had been taught. Twenty years ago, as a young man in London, he had given evidence at the Old Bailey in a case of forged banknotes and had been told by a judge to speak up, and he had spoken up ever since.

The coroner heard Pike, and Jory, and the boy Kit, who spoke so quietly that nobody could hear him at all. The Reverend Thorne said that Mr Lusk had been a sober and honest man. Nobody mentioned the empty pockets.

The jury returned a verdict of accidental death by drowning, and the coroner thanked them and caught the afternoon coach.
