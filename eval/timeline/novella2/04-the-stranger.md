# Chapter 4

On the evening of the fourth, Constable Pike stood in the mill kitchen of the Weir House and looked at the stranger laid out on Nell Garrow's table.

The Reverend Silas Thorne, the vicar of Wendle, had come down with him, as the vicar always came where there was a death. He looked at the dead man's face for a long while.

"His name is Lusk," said the Reverend Thorne. "Matthew Lusk. A cattle dealer from Tavy Cross. He wrote to me last month about the glebe meadow."

Pike went through the dead man's pockets and found a wet letter addressed to the Weir House, unopened, and no money at all.

"A man who pays for brandy with a half-sovereign," said Pike, "and drowns with empty pockets."

Nell said nothing. She was looking at the letter with her own name on it.
