# Chapter 2

The next morning the river had dropped a foot, and Kit was out on the weir before his mother was dressed, walking the slippery top of it with his arms out like a rope-dancer.

He found the man caught in the boards of the weir below the sluice, face down, in a good brown coat. Kit did not touch him. He ran for Jory the ferryman, who kept his boat at the foot of the mill-race, and Jory came with a boat-hook and a face like a closed door.

Nell knew the weir. In the flood of 1868 it had taken her husband, Tom Garrow, who went out at night to free the sluice gate and was found in the same boards three days later. She had not let Kit near the weir for a year after, and she had never told him how his father was found.

Jory and Kit brought the stranger up onto the bank and laid him on a hurdle. Nell covered his face with her own shawl and sent Kit running to the village for the constable.
