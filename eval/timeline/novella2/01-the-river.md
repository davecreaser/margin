# Chapter 1

Thursday the third of March, 1881, was the day the Wendle rose higher than anyone in the village could remember. Nell Garrow, the miller's widow, watched it from the door of the Weir House all afternoon, a shawl over her head and her hands in her apron.

By evening the water was over the lower meadow and still climbing. Her son Kit, who was fourteen and believed himself a man, went out three times to look at the sluice, and three times she called him back.

"It'll take the bridge," Kit said.

"Hollin Bridge has three arches and has stood two hundred years," said Nell. "It'll take the hens first."

That same night, a little before eleven, they heard it go. It was not a crash, as Kit had imagined, but a long grinding groan, like a beast lying down, and then nothing but the river. Nell stood at the window with the candle and saw, by the light of a lantern on the far bank, the place where the middle arch had been.
