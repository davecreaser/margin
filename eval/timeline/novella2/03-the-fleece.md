# Chapter 3

On the night of the third of March, Constable Ambrose Pike was sitting in the taproom of the Fleece with a pint of mild, which he considered part of his duties, when a stranger in a brown coat came in out of the rain and asked the way to the Weir House.

The landlord told him to cross Hollin Bridge and follow the mill-race. The stranger paid for a glass of brandy with a half-sovereign, would not wait for his change, and went out again into the rain.

Pike watched him go. He did not like men who would not wait for their change.

A little before eleven the whole taproom heard the bridge go down. Pike was first to the door, and by the light of the landlord's lantern he saw the black gap where the middle arch had been, and the river pouring through it white.
