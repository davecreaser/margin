# Llandudno Again

Years later Denry took Nellie to Llandudno to show her the scenes of his first fortune. He pointed out the beach where his lifeboat had run its trips. Cregeen had sold him the lifeboat for fifty pounds, he told her, and it had paid for itself in a week.

"And the photographs," he said. "Copies at a shilling each, handsomely mounted. Everybody bought one."

They walked past the boarding-house where he had once stayed at thirty shillings a week, and the landlady, a Mrs Prowse, came out to shake his hand and remind him that he still owed her for a broken jug.

He told Nellie how his public had given sixty-eight pounds to the Lifeboat Institution, and how the slowness of the Hjalmar in wrecking itself had cost him a loss of seven hundred pounds. Nellie said that she had never heard of anybody losing money because a ship was not wrecked fast enough. Denry said that was because she had not known many financiers.
