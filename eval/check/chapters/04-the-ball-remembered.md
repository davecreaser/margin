# The Ball Remembered

Nellie liked to hear about the ball, and Denry told it well. He had been Mr Duncalf's clerk then, earning twenty-five shillings a week, and he had learnt to write shorthand at a hundred and twenty words a minute because Mr Duncalf had insisted on it.

"There were fifty thousand inhabitants in Bursley," he said, "and not one of them believed I would dare."

Ruth Earp, then only nineteen, had been there too, the best dancer in the Five Towns. But it was Denry who had walked across the floor, and Denry who had been the first to dance with the Countess, before any alderman, and the town had never forgotten it.

The band at the ball, he said, had been led by a cornet player from Hanbridge who played every waltz too fast. Nellie said she remembered the cornet player and that he was her uncle. Denry was not sure whether to believe her.
