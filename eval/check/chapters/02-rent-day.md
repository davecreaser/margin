# Rent Day

Monday was rent day, and Denry went round Mrs Codleyn's cottages with his book. The cottages brought her in about fifteen pounds a week gross, and Denry collected the rents for five per cent, which she still thought too much.

He stopped first at the Widow Hullins's. The old woman had brought up nine children in that cottage, and she spoke of each of them as though they were still at home. Denry had bought the cottage for sixty pounds, a fact the whole town seemed to know.

Mrs Codleyn kept a grey parrot that could say "Rates!" in a tone of real bitterness. Denry thought it the most sensible creature in her house.

At the end of the round he remembered Ruth Earp, who had once owed fifty pounds in rent and had tried to move her furniture by night. He did not call on her. Some debts are better left in the book.
