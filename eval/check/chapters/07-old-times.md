# Old Times

One winter evening Denry sat with his mother in the cottage and let her tell him things he knew. He had been born in 1867, in Brougham Street, she said, and he had been an anxiety from the first day.

"And now there's the mule," she said. "A mule aged seven, and you paid good money for it."

Denry said that the mule was a sound investment, like everything else, and reminded her of Llandudno, where the Hjalmar had been so slow in wrecking itself that it had cost him a loss of four hundred pounds, and how he had made it all back and more.

"Thirty-five thousand inhabitants in Bursley," said his mother, "and not one of them with any sense but me."

She went to put the kettle on. Denry looked at the clock on the mantelpiece, which had stopped at twenty past four years ago and which she would not have mended.
