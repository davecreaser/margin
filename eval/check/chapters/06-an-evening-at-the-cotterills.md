# An Evening at the Cotterills'

Denry went to supper at the Cotterills' on a wet Thursday. Nellie Cotterill opened the door herself, because the servant had gone to bed with toothache.

Mrs Cotterill said again that she could run a house without a servant and have herself tidy by ten o'clock in a morning. Mr Cotterill said nothing; he was thinking about his tomatoes, which he grew in a lean-to behind the house and would let no one else water.

Over supper they spoke of the Widow Hullins, who had brought up eleven children, and of Shillitoe, the tailor, whose trade was growing every year. Denry told the story of the pantechnicon that had passed him at three and a half miles an hour, which was not fast, he said, until you were on it.

Nellie laughed at all the right places. Her mother noticed, and said nothing, and poured the tea.
