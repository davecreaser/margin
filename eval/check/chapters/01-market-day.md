# Market Day

On the first Saturday of the month Denry drove into St Luke's Market behind his mule. The mule, aged twelve and as wise as any alderman, knew the road better than its master did. It had cost Denry twenty pounds, a sum he mentioned whenever anybody laughed at it.

He had harnessed it to the little old victoria that had cost him ten pounds, and the whole turn-out, with the good set of harness bought for five guineas, drew a small crowd outside the Town Hall.

Denry had named the mule Gaffer, though nobody in Bursley ever called it anything but "Denry's mule." A blacksmith called Wedgwood Pidduck shod it every quarter and never sent his bill on time.

His mother watched from the doorway of the cottage. She could wash flannel with less shrinking than any other woman in the district, and she did not approve of mules, or of markets, or of being looked at.

"You'll be the talk of the town," she said.

"I am the talk of the town," said Denry.
